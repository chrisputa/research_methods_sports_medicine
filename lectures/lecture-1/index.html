Learning objectives:

  • Name the principal performance-diagnostic procedures in sports medicine and what each of them measures
  • Reproduce the stepwise diagnostic model from history to training recommendation
  • Apply the three guiding questions of ergometric diagnostics to a concrete referral
  • Separate direct physical quantities, biological surrogate parameters and subjective ratings

1 What Sports Medical Diagnostics Is For

Sports medicine uses a wide range of medical methods for functional diagnostics in healthy and in diseased people. Two goals run in parallel and are rarely cleanly separable: health assessment — is there a condition that makes a given load unsafe? — and performance assessment — what is this person currently capable of, and where are the limits? The most important single procedure is spiroergometry, frequently combined with lactate performance diagnostics. Central to both is the determination of performance at defined points of energy supply, read from ventilation, gas exchange, heart rate regulation and blood lactate concentration, supplemented by the subjective reports of the person tested and the judgement of the examining physician (Puta & Gabriel 2024).

The two most common set-ups are cycle and treadmill ergometry within an exercise ECG. They yield statements about current capacity and about plausible performance limits in relation to covariates such as age, sex and health status, and they permit both general statements (overall fitness) and partial ones at the level of organ systems, organs and cells.

2 How Physical Performance Becomes Assessable

Assessment rests on quantitative measures of three different kinds, and confusing them is the most common source of over-interpretation in student reports:

  • Direct physical quantities — height, distance, time, velocity, power. These are what performance is.
  • Biological surrogate parameters — heart rate, metabolic variables, blood markers. These stand in for internal load and are always one inferential step removed.
  • Subjective ratings — rank orders and scale values given by the person or by third parties.

Reference ranges generated from such investigations carry an orienting, not a diagnostic, significance in practice. A value outside a reference range is a prompt for a question, not an answer.

3 The Stepwise Model

Sports medical diagnostics proceeds in steps, and each step is only entered if the previous one has not already answered the question. The ladder runs: history → physical findings → instrument-based diagnostics → performance diagnostics → therapy recommendations → training recommendations. The ordering is economic as well as epistemic: most of the diagnostic yield sits in the first two rungs, and the expensive rungs are justified by what the cheap ones found.

4 The Three Guiding Questions

Before an ergometric test is ordered, three questions are answered explicitly:

  1. Why should ergometric capacity be measured?
  2. Is the measurement and assessment of ergometric capacity necessary and/or sensible?
  3. Which methods and which parameters are appropriate for assessing capacity?

The answers differ systematically by context. In medical diagnostics the aim is a diagnosis and the selection criterion is suspicion or course of disease; in competitive sport the aim is a performance diagnosis with sport- and competition-specific procedures; in research the study question governs; in teaching the curriculum does; and in the case of purely personal interest the aim is the satisfaction of that interest with simple, evidence-based procedures. Resources, the interest of the person and their environment, and normative requirements act as selection criteria across all contexts.

5 The Method Inventory

One-Minute-Paper Topics

A One-Minute-Paper (OMP) is a short, focused prompt that students answer in ~60 seconds at the end of a session to consolidate learning, surface misconceptions, and provide formative feedback. When answering, be concise, specific, and use terminology from today’s session.

  • A 19-year-old handball player wants “a lactate test because everyone gets one”. Answer the three guiding questions for this case and state whether the test is indicated.
  • Classify heart rate, power output and the Borg scale into the three kinds of measure in §2, and name one interpretive risk for each.
  • Why is the stepwise model ordered as it is? Give one clinical and one economic argument.
